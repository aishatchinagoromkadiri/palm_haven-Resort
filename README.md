# Palm Haven Hotels & Resorts: Business Analysis

*A capstone project using a simulated hotel group case. I led Team 4, a four-person team within the capstone group, and built the dashboards and wrote the report shown here.*

## The problem
Palm Haven is a hotel group with five properties across Southern Nigeria. Over 12 months, its repeat booking rate fell from 41% to 26%, corporate account retention fell from 88% to 71%, and guest satisfaction fell from 4.3 to 3.6 out of 5. Two corporate accounts worth ₦340m a year left.

Management believed the physical product was the problem and was considering a ₦1.8bn refurbishment. The task was to test that belief against the evidence before the money was spent.

## My contribution
- Built a Power BI dashboard on **10,000 guest stay records**
- Built a second Power BI dashboard on **200 customer survey responses**
- Compared what the two sources showed
- Wrote the report with findings and recommendations

## Key findings
1. **The stay data showed almost no difference between properties.** Repeat rates ranged only from 69.9% to 74.1% across all five properties, so no single property stood out as the problem.
2. **The survey pointed somewhere else.** Benin City and Ikeja scored worst on service consistency and intent to return. In Benin City, 95% of respondents said service was inconsistent and 80% said they would not return, compared with 69% and 66% at Victoria Island.
3. **Interviews named a third pair of properties.** Stakeholder interviews pointed to Victoria Island and Abuja as the two most affected. So three sources disagreed about where the problem is.
4. **A past refurbishment did not last.** After the 2023 Ikeja refurbishment (₦420m), satisfaction rose from 3.8 to 4.1 for two quarters, then fell back to 3.7 within 12 months. The official review found that maintenance discipline never changed.
5. **The proposed ₦1.8bn budget was for capital upgrades, not maintenance.** Finance and Engineering confirmed it would not restore the engineering team or the maintenance programme, which was cut 11 months before the investigation.

## Recommendations
1. **Pause the ₦1.8bn refurbishment** until the real cause is confirmed. The past Ikeja refurbishment suggests it may not fix the problem on its own.
2. **Restore maintenance discipline first.** Bring back preventive maintenance and set up faster complaint handling, then measure whether repeat bookings and satisfaction improve.
3. **Confirm which properties are really affected** before making property-level decisions, since the three sources disagreed.
4. **Fix how the group collects data.** Only 11% of guests complete the survey and maintenance complaints are recorded only when raised at the front desk. Collecting repeat rate and satisfaction by property and by issue type would show the real picture.
5. **Enforce the same rates on direct and OTA channels.** Guests were finding cheaper OTA rates than direct ones, and rate parity was not being monitored.

## Limitations
- This is a simulated case, and the stay data is a sample.
- The sources disagreed, so these findings show where more evidence is needed, not a final answer.
- Correlation is not cause: complaints and repeat decline moving together does not prove one causes the other.
